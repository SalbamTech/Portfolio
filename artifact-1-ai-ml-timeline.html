Description

Progress is a chain, not a single breakthrough.

Modern generative AI rests on decades of work in computation, learning theory, neural networks, data, specialized hardware, and human-computer interaction. The timeline highlights turning points that changed what machines could do and how people understood their capabilities.

Reading lensEach milestone is framed by both its technical contribution and its larger significance. The goal is not merely to memorize dates, but to see how research ideas become systems—and how systems create new social and professional responsibilities.

Tangible artifact

The AI & ML timeline

  1. 1950

    The imitation game

    Alan Turing’s “Computing Machinery and Intelligence” reframed the question “Can machines think?” as an observable test of machine behavior, establishing a lasting foundation for debate about intelligence.

  2. 1956

    Artificial intelligence becomes a field

    The Dartmouth Summer Research Project brought researchers together around the proposition that aspects of learning and intelligence could be precisely described and simulated by machines.

  3. 1986

    Backpropagation strengthens neural learning

    Rumelhart, Hinton, and Williams described a learning procedure that repeatedly adjusts network weights to reduce error, helping hidden units learn useful internal representations.

  4. 1997

    Deep Blue defeats the world chess champion

    IBM’s Deep Blue defeated Garry Kasparov under standard tournament controls, demonstrating the power of specialized computation, search, evaluation functions, and domain knowledge.

  5. 2012

    AlexNet accelerates deep learning

    A large convolutional neural network trained on ImageNet achieved a decisive improvement in image-classification performance, showing the combined value of large datasets, GPUs, and deep architectures.

  6. 2017

    The Transformer changes sequence modeling

    “Attention Is All You Need” introduced an architecture based on attention rather than recurrence or convolution, enabling highly parallel training and becoming the foundation of modern large language models.

  7. 2020

    Few-shot language models scale

    GPT-3 demonstrated that a sufficiently large language model could perform many tasks from instructions and examples in its text context, without task-specific gradient updates.

  8. 2022

    Conversational generative AI reaches the public

    ChatGPT’s research preview made dialogue with a large language model broadly accessible, while also making limitations, misuse risks, and the need for human oversight more visible.

Process

How the artifact was developed

  1. Define the storyOrganize the timeline around shifts in ideas, learning methods, computing scale, and access.
  2. Verify the evidenceUse original papers and institutional sources to confirm dates, claims, and significance.
  3. Select deliberatelyChoose milestones that explain the path to contemporary AI without pretending the history is exhaustive.
  4. Translate clearlyCondense technical contributions into accurate language for mixed technical and nontechnical audiences.
  5. Design for scanningUse consistent dates, short explanations, strong hierarchy, and responsive layout.
  6. Add responsibilityConnect technical capability to governance, limitations, security, and human accountability.

Value proposition of the artifact

Why this work matters

Unique value

The timeline combines technical history with an implementation-minded perspective. It shows that I can synthesize complex evidence, identify meaningful turning points, and communicate how earlier research shapes today’s opportunities and risks.

Relevance

Technology and security leaders need professionals who understand that current AI capabilities are part of an evolving system—not magic. That context supports better expectation-setting, risk assessment, governance, and adoption decisions.
