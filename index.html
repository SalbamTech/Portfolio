ARTIFACT / 02

Machine Learning vs. Deep Learning: Real-World Case Analysis

AIML-500 · Assignment 2.3 — Machine Learning vs. Deep Learning

Introduction

A comparative case study showing how data type, explainability, computing requirements, and operational risk determine whether traditional machine learning or deep learning is the better approach.

ObjectiveDifferentiate machine learning from deep learning and justify the most suitable approach for two real-world problems.
AudienceSecurity leaders, AI project stakeholders, technical hiring managers, and implementation teams.
FormatAnalytical report and web-based case-study brief.
RelevanceDemonstrates practical model-selection judgment instead of choosing technology based only on novelty.
Description

The first case examines Apache SpamAssassin, where Bayesian filtering and understandable email signals support a traditional machine-learning approach. This method fits organizations that need lower computing costs, transparent decisions, and the ability to update a model from user-labeled messages.

The second case examines deep-learning image recognition for diabetic-retinopathy screening. A convolutional neural network can learn subtle visual patterns directly from retinal photographs, making it more suitable than a conventional model that depends on manually engineered image features. The comparison also addresses clinical validation, monitoring, and the need for qualified human review.

Process

I compared each case across feature extraction, data volume, computing requirements, explainability, deployment conditions, error consequences, and human oversight. I then used technical and clinical evidence to justify the selected approach and explain why the alternative was less suitable.

ARTIFACT / 03

Neural Networks, Explained Simply

AIML-500 · Discussion — Communication for Learning

Introduction

A plain-language explanation of neural networks designed to make a complex machine-learning concept understandable, memorable, and useful to a nontechnical audience.

ObjectiveExplain how neural networks learn while demonstrating communication strategies that support organizational learning.
AudienceBusiness leaders, change teams, new AI learners, and nontechnical stakeholders.
FormatDiscussion post revised as an interactive web-based learning brief.
RelevanceAI initiatives succeed when technical ideas can be translated into language that supports informed decisions and adoption.
Description

The artifact compares a neural network to a student learning to recognize a cat. It then connects that familiar story to the actual training cycle: receiving examples, detecting patterns, making a prediction, measuring error, adjusting internal connections, and repeating the process.

  • Chunking: separates the training process into six digestible steps.
  • Analogy: connects an abstract system to a familiar learning experience.
  • Progressive disclosure: introduces technical details only after establishing the main idea.
  • Balanced framing: explains both real-world value and limitations related to biased or poor-quality data.

The revised version adds a visual learning pathway, a concise takeaway, examples of practical applications, and a section that distinguishes pattern learning from human understanding.

Process

I drafted a detailed technical explanation, used generative AI to test alternative analogies and organization, then revised the content for accuracy, accessibility, and audience relevance. I removed unnecessary jargon, corrected overstatements about learning from individual errors, and retained essential concepts such as layers, feedback, generalization, and data quality.

ARTIFACT / 05

AI-Powered Chatbot Design & Implementation Plan

AIML-500 · Assignment 1.4 — AI Lab, Part 2

Introduction

An end-to-end design-thinking application: planning, prototyping, and testing an AI chatbot from user empathy through deployment guardrails.

ObjectiveDesign and plan an AI chatbot using the complete design-thinking methodology.
AudienceProduct teams, AI practitioners, and business stakeholders.
FormatComprehensive plan document plus working implementation (Chatbase).
RelevanceChatbots are the most common first AI deployment in organizations; this plan models how to do it responsibly.
Description

Empathy: identified users (support staff, customers), their pain points (repetitive inquiries, 24/7 availability gaps), and their needs (quick resolution, knowledge accessibility).

Definition: articulated the problem statement and mapped user needs to AI/ML capabilities (classification, retrieval, response generation) alongside realistic limitations (e.g., complex emotional support remains human work).

Ideation: brainstormed multiple chatbot architectures — rule-based, retrieval-augmented, and fully generative — evaluating feasibility, cost, and user-experience trade-offs.

Prototyping & testing: selected Chatbase as the platform, defined system prompts, set guardrails, planned test scenarios (happy path, edge cases, boundary conditions), and designed monitoring for bias and drift.

Process

I moved through each design-thinking phase in sequence, documenting decisions and trade-offs at every step, then built and tested a working prototype against the planned scenarios before finalizing the implementation plan.

Reflective Insights

Artifact 3 strengthened a different part of my professional practice: communicating technical knowledge so that others can use it. Revising my neural-network explanation showed me that simplification is not the removal of important ideas. It is the deliberate selection, sequencing, and framing of those ideas for a particular audience. Artifact 2 moved my portfolio from ethical reflection into technical decision-making. Comparing a transparent email classifier with a deep image-recognition system helped me understand that model selection is not about choosing the newest method. It is about matching the method to the data, operating environment, risk, and people who remain responsible for the result.

This portfolio represents more than course completion — it documents a deliberate journey into the intersection of technology and human values. As I've engaged with AI and machine learning fundamentals, I've become more convinced that the most pressing questions aren't technical; they're human.

What I've learned: machines can imitate the mechanics of reasoning without possessing its meaning. This gap is not a limitation to overcome; it's the essential frontier where human judgment, ethics, and creativity remain irreplaceable. My role as a technical professional is to build systems that amplify human capability while respecting the boundaries of machine intelligence.

Going forward: I'm committed to advancing my technical credentials (CISSP, SC-200, advanced ML coursework) while never losing sight of the people, organizations, and communities my work affects. This portfolio is a starting point — a resource for others learning to integrate AI responsibly, and a record of the thinking that will guide my continued growth as a professional and leader in emerging technologies.
